Sand Earth

Landforms repeat. The same uplift, erosion and rivers carve the same shapes on every continent. So the ridge you press into clay on a café table very likely exists somewhere, at full size.

How it works.

Three steps from a handful of sand to a real place on the map.

  1. Step 1

    Sculpt.

    Press hills, ridges and valleys into sand or modelling clay. Big, clear shapes work best.

  2. Step 2

    Photograph.

    Take one photo from straight above. A depth model turns it into a height map on your Mac.

  3. Step 3

    Found.

    Your shape is compared with 88,531 real places, turned through every angle, and shown on satellite imagery and in 3D.

Tested, not just pretty.

We take real places, bend them into rough, rotated, blurry copies like a hand-made sculpture, and check whether the search traces each one back.

About five minutes on a Mac with Python 3.10 or newer.

git clone https://github.com/Tilakkumar13/sand2earth
cd sand2earth
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python build_index.py --zoom 6 --name world_z6
python app.py --index data/world_z6

About the project.

An experiment where GIS, computer vision and remote sensing meet: a physical sculpture goes in, a real place on the planet comes out.

Why it exists.

Projected sandboxes can draw contour lines on sand, and tangible GIS tools can rebuild a known place in sand. None of them asks the reverse question: given a shape made by hand, where on Earth is it?

What's inside.

Depth Anything V2 reads the photo as a height map. A neural network trained on thousands of real landscapes picks the candidates. Every candidate is then turned through all angles and compared exactly, which also tells you which way your sandbox would face on the map.

A fair map of the planet.

Web Mercator stretches the poles, so pixel-sized windows make Greenland win far too often. Here every window is measured in kilometres, giving every latitude the same chance.

Where the data comes from.

Elevation from the open AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM, ETOPO1 and national surveys). Imagery from Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX. Place names from OpenStreetMap.

Standing on the shoulders of.

  • Augmented Reality SandboxKreylos, UC Davis. Depth camera and projected contours.
  • Tangible LandscapeNC State. Sculpt sand to match a known place.
  • Content-based landscape retrievalJasiewicz, Netzel and Stepinski, 2013.
  • LSE WisconsinHaffner et al., 2023. Neural landscape search, regional.
  • GEAR2026. Siamese retrieval of terrain analogues.
  • terravectorDEM patch similarity search.
